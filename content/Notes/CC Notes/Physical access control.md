Physical access control focuses on protecting the tangible assets of an organization—people, hardware, and facilities—by restricting who can enter or exit a physical space.

- **Purpose:** To prevent unauthorized physical access to sensitive areas like data centers, wiring closets, and executive offices.
- **Importance:** Even the strongest firewall is useless if an attacker can walk into a server room and physically steal a hard drive or plant a hardware keylogger.

### **Common Physical Controls**

- **Barriers:** Fences, gates, and bollards to define the perimeter.
- **Entry Controls:** Locks (key or electronic), badge readers, and "mantraps" (a double-door entry system where the first door must close before the second opens).
- **Surveillance:** CCTV cameras and motion sensors to monitor and record activity.
- **Personnel:** Security guards and receptionists who verify IDs and manage visitor logs.
- **Hardware Protection:** Locked server racks, cable locks for laptops, and port covers to prevent unauthorized USB devices.
