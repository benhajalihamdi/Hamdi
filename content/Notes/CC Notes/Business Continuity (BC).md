**Business Continuity (BC):**
is an organization’s ability to ensure that critical business functions continue to operate—or are restored to a functional state as quickly as possible—following a massive disruption. This could be anything from a natural disaster (hurricane) to a cyberattack (ransomware) or a global pandemic.

### 1. The Purpose of BC

The primary goal is to **keep the business alive.**

- **Resilience:** Building systems that can withstand a hit without failing.
- **Speed:** Ensuring the "downtime" is as short as possible.
- **Safety:** Prioritizing the lives and safety of employees and customers.

### 2. The Importance of BC

Without a solid BC plan, a single event can lead to a total business collapse.

- **Financial Survival:** Avoiding the massive costs of idle staff, missed sales, and regulatory fines.
- **Reputation:** Maintaining customer trust. If a bank’s app is down for three days, customers will leave.
- **Legal Compliance:** Many industries (healthcare, finance) are legally required to have a plan to keep services available.

### 3. Core Components of BC

A successful Business Continuity strategy is built on several key pillars:
#### **Business Impact Analysis (BIA)**

This is the "investigation" phase. You identify which business processes are most critical and what would happen if they stopped.

- **RTO (Recovery Time Objective):** How quickly must we be back up?
- **RPO (Recovery Point Objective):** How much data can we afford to lose?
#### **Disaster Recovery (DR) Plan**

Often confused with BC, DR is actually a _subset_ of BC. It focuses specifically on the IT and technical side, restoring servers, recovering backups, and getting the network online.
#### **Crisis Management and Communication**

This is the "human" element. It defines who is in charge during a disaster, how employees will be notified, and what the public message will be.
