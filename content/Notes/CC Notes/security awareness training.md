title: Security Awareness Training
---
The goal of awareness training is to change user behavior from a security risk to a security asset.

- **Social Engineering:** Training teaches users to recognize psychological manipulation. This includes **Phishing** (fraudulent emails), **Vishing** (voice scams), **Pretexting** (creating fake scenarios), and **Tailgating** (following someone into a secure building).
- **Password Protection:** Educating users on "password hygiene," such as using complex passphrases, avoiding password reuse across sites, and the proper use of password managers.
- **Data Handling:** Reminding employees how to identify sensitive data and the proper way to share it (e.g., not using personal email or unencrypted USB drives).
- **Physical Security:** Concepts like the "Clean Desk Policy" (not leaving sensitive info out) and the importance of locking workstations when walking away.

### **Importance**

Technology alone cannot stop every attack; a well-trained workforce acts as a "human firewall."

- **Reducing Risk:** Most breaches (over 80% in many studies) involve a human element, such as clicking a malicious link. Training directly targets this vulnerability.
- **Compliance:** Many regulations (HIPAA, PCI-DSS, GDPR) specifically require documented security awareness training for all staff.
- **Incident Detection:** Employees are often the first to notice something "weird." Training empowers them to report suspicious activity to the Incident Response team quickly.
- **Cost-Effectiveness:** It is often significantly cheaper to train a user not to click a link than it is to clean up a ransomware infection or pay a legal fine for a data breach.
