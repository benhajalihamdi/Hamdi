**Incident Response (IR)** is the organized approach an organization takes to address and manage the aftermath of a security breach or cyberattack. If a **Disaster Recovery** plan handles a hurricane, an **Incident Response** plan handles a hacker in the system.

## 1. The Purpose of IR

The core mission of IR is to handle a "security event" in a way that limits damage and reduces recovery time and costs.

- **Rapid Detection:** Identifying a threat as soon as it enters the environment.
- **Eradication:** Completely removing the threat (malware, unauthorized user, etc.) from the network.
- **Learning:** Using the "post-mortem" to ensure the same vulnerability isn't exploited twice.
## 2. The Importance of IR

- **Minimizing Financial Loss:** Every hour a breach goes uncontained, it costs the company money in lost data, productivity, and potential ransoms.
- **Legal and Regulatory Compliance:** Laws like GDPR require companies to report breaches within a specific timeframe (usually 72 hours). Without an IR plan, you can’t meet these deadlines.
- **Evidence Preservation:** A disorganized response can destroy digital evidence. Proper IR ensures that "forensics" are preserved for law enforcement or insurance claims.
